Chinese EV Parts Wholesale: Battery Cooling, E-Axle, OBC & High-Voltage Components — 2026 Guide

Why Chinese EV Parts Demand Is Surging in Emerging Markets

Electric vehicle sales are no longer a story about Europe and North America. IEA data puts global EV sales above 17 million units in 2025, with China accounting for well over half of that total. In the same year BYD, Geely, SAIC, and Chery exported roughly 1.4 million vehicles combined, and Counterpoint Research tracks Chinese brands at over 60% of EV imports into Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Latin America. Every one of those vehicles needs replacement parts within its first 36 months of service.

The problem for parts importers is that these vehicles use high-voltage architecture that traditional auto parts distributors do not stock. A BYD Dolphin or Atto 3, a Geely Geometry, or an MG4 runs a battery cooling plate, an integrated e-axle drive unit, an on-board charger, and high-voltage wiring that a conventional Toyota or Hyundai parts catalog simply does not cover. Chinese manufacturers built this supply chain from the ground up between 2020 and 2025, and the Guangzhou parts cluster where Kingmay operates is the largest export base for these components.

This guide covers the four highest-demand EV parts categories for importers, with OE cross-reference data, FOB Guangzhou pricing, certification requirements by market, and container shipping plans. It is written for EV importers, fleet operators, and independent workshops that service Chinese-brand electric vehicles in emerging markets.

Battery Cooling Plates & Thermal Management

The battery cooling plate is the flat liquid-circulation panel that sits between a battery pack's cells and its cooling loop. It is the single most replaced thermal component on Chinese EVs in hot-climate markets, because the same 45°C ambient temperature that shortens an air conditioner's life in Saudi Arabia also forces the battery cooling system to run at full load for hours at a time. A cooling plate that leaks or clogs in a BYD, Geely, or GAC battery pack does not trigger a warning light — it quietly raises cell temperature, cuts charging speed, and shortens pack life.

Chinese suppliers produce cooling plates in three material grades. The entry grade uses stamped aluminum with a single serpentine channel and sells below $140 FOB; the mid grade uses friction-stir-welded 3003 aluminum with dual channels and sells at $150-$200; the top grade uses vacuum-brazed 6061 aluminum with 8-12 parallel micro-channels and sells above $200. For hot-climate importers the friction-stir-welded dual-channel plate is the value sweet spot, because it balances thermal performance against cost.

E-Axle Electric Drive Units: Motor, Gearbox & Inverter

The e-axle is the integrated electric drive unit that combines the traction motor, a single-speed reduction gearbox, and the inverter into one housing. It replaces the engine, transmission, and differential of a combustion vehicle in a single module, which is why it is the highest-value single EV component an importer can stock. When an e-axle fails — typically a bearing, an IGBT module, or a rotor position sensor — the repair is almost always a full unit swap rather than a component rebuild, because the housing is sealed and the high-voltage parts are not serviceable in a general workshop.

Chinese e-axle production is led by BYD FinDreams, Huawei, and Nidec, backed by a cluster of Guangzhou and Shenzhen suppliers that build to the same platform specifications. The three output classes that matter for importers are 70-110 kW for compact cars, 110-160 kW for midsize crossovers, and 160-250 kW for performance and 800V models. FOB pricing tracks output class more closely than brand, because the motor's copper, magnets, and silicon content set the cost floor.

When sourcing an e-axle, the buyer has to confirm four specifications before quoting: output power (kW), peak torque (Nm), voltage architecture (400V or 800V), and the mounting interface, which differs between front-wheel-drive, rear-wheel-drive, and all-wheel-drive platforms. A rear-drive BYD Seal e-axle will not bolt into a front-drive Geometry E, even at the same power rating. Kingmay's engineering team provides an interface matching sheet with every e-axle quote so importers can confirm fitment against the host vehicle's platform code before committing.

On-Board Chargers (OBC) & DC-DC Converters

The on-board charger converts AC power from a wall outlet or public charger into the DC current that charges the traction battery, and the DC-DC converter steps the battery's high voltage down to the 12V level that runs the vehicle's lights, screens, and control modules. Both are sealed power-electronics modules that fail from the same root causes: water ingress, grid voltage spikes, and sustained high-temperature operation. In markets with unstable grid power — Nigeria, Kenya, and parts of Southeast Asia — the OBC is the most frequently replaced electronics component on Chinese EVs after the first 24 months.

OBCs are specified by charging power (3.3 kW, 6.6 kW, 7.2 kW, or 11 kW), input phase (single or three-phase), and output voltage (400V or 800V). The 6.6 kW single-phase unit is the volume part for emerging markets, because it charges a 40-50 kWh pack overnight on a standard 220V outlet without requiring three-phase infrastructure that many importers' customers do not have. The 11 kW and 22 kW three-phase units serve commercial fleets and markets with three-phase supply.

The DC-DC converter is smaller and cheaper but no less critical — when it fails, the vehicle still drives but the 12V systems drain until the car will not restart. Chinese suppliers sell DC-DC converters in 1.5 kW, 2.5 kW, and 3.3 kW output classes, with 2.5 kW as the standard replacement part for most compact and midsize Chinese EVs.

High-Voltage Wiring, Connectors & Safety Components

High-voltage components carry current at 300-800V, which makes them a safety-critical category that importers treat differently from 12V auto electrics. The category covers high-voltage wiring harnesses, charging-port assemblies, HV connectors, contactors, fuses, and service-disconnect plugs. Every one of these parts carries an interlock circuit that shuts the pack down if a connector is loose or damaged, and every one must be sourced with its matching seal and terminal kit rather than as a bare wire or housing.

For importers the practical entry point is the charging-port assembly and the high-voltage wiring harness, because both are collision-damage and wear parts rather than electronics. A front-end collision on a BYD Atto 3 or MG4 often takes out the charging port and the adjacent HV harness even when the battery and e-axle are untouched, so body shops and EV workshops buy these parts in volume. High-voltage connectors and contactors are smaller-ticket items but move steadily as maintenance parts.

Safety note: High-voltage parts must never be handled, tested, or installed with standard 12V workshop tools or by untrained staff. Always ship HV connectors with their interlock terminals, specify the correct voltage class (400V or 800V), and include the orange HV cable and warning labels in every order. Kingmay supplies HV parts only with the matching insulation, sealing, and interlock hardware in the same carton.

EV parts cross the same customs lines as any auto parts, but the lithium battery content in some modules and the high-voltage classification of others add two extra requirements: UN38.3 testing for any part that ships with or near a battery cell, and voltage-class documentation for anything that operates above 60V DC. FAQ: Chinese EV Parts Wholesale from China

What is the minimum order quantity for Chinese EV parts?

MOQ varies by category: e-axle drive units are one unit, battery cooling plates and DC-DC converters start at 10 pieces, OBCs at 5 pieces, and wiring harnesses at 5 pieces. A balanced opening order that covers the four main categories typically runs $8,000-$15,000 FOB, which qualifies for consolidated LCL shipping.

Do Chinese EV parts fit BYD, Geely, MG and other brands?

Yes. Kingmay stocks OEM-grade EV parts for the top Chinese EV platforms — BYD e-platform 3.0 and CTB, Geely Geometry, GAC Aion, MG (SAIC), Chery, GWM Ora, Changan Deepal, Neta, Leapmotor, Wuling, and XPeng. Each part is matched to the host vehicle platform code so importers can confirm fitment before ordering.

How do I verify the quality of an EV part before buying?

Request three documents: the IATF 16949 certificate of the factory, the UN38.3 test report for battery-adjacent modules, and the voltage-class documentation for high-voltage parts. Kingmay provides all three with every order, along with pre-shipment photos and, on request, a third-party inspection report for orders above $10,000.

Are battery cooling plates and OBCs safe to ship by sea?

Yes. Cooling plates and OBCs that do not contain lithium cells ship as general cargo. Only modules that ship with or near battery cells — such as a complete battery pack or a BMS with an integrated cell — are classified as UN3480/UN3481 dangerous goods and require the UN38.3 report and a DG-certified container booking. Kingmay marks every carton with the correct class so the shipment clears customs without delay.

Can you supply parts for 800V platforms?

Yes. Kingmay supplies vacuum-brazed micro-channel cooling plates, 800V e-axle units, and 800V-rated OBCs and HV connectors for platforms such as BYD Seal, XPeng G6, and Changan Deepal. 800V parts are priced above their 400V equivalents because of the higher-specification insulation and silicon content, and they must be matched to the correct voltage class on every order.

How long does delivery take to the Middle East or Africa?

From order confirmation to arrival, budget 25-30 days to Jebel Ali or Mombasa, 30-35 days to Lagos, and 17-21 days to Jeddah, including order processing, UN38.3 documentation, and LCL consolidation. Thailand and Southeast Asia are fastest at 8-12 days total. Bulk 20GP shipments for repeat importers reduce the lead time by three to five days.
