BYD Sealion 7 and Sealion 5 in 2026: What Each Costs FOB Nansha, and Where It Actually Sells

📅 2026-11-26 ⏱ 15 min read ✍ Globalucar Editorial

BYD sells three different SUVs under one name, and that confusion costs dealers about US$7,600 a unit. A buyer who searches BYD Sealion 7 export and gets quoted a Sealion 5 has been sold the wrong car: same badge family, different platform, 4,700 mm against 4,830 mm, 115 km of electric range against 150 km, and a US$7,590 FOB gap at Nansha. This guide lines up the three 2026 Sealion export specs we actually hold in Guangzhou, prices each one FOB Nansha against its FCA invoice, then carries the Sealion 7 landed cost through Dubai, Lagos and Fremantle so the figure you quote a customer survives customs.

Three Sealions, One Badge, Three Different Cars

In Chinese the nameplate reads 海狮 — sea lion — and it sits inside BYD's Ocean series next to the Seal sedan and the Dolphin hatchback. The export market compressed all of them into a single search term, which is why enquiries for a “Sealion” arrive with no displacement, no range and no drive type attached. Three separate vehicles answer to it, and only one of them is a battery-electric car.

The Sealion 5 (海狮05 DM-i) is the compact one: 4,700 mm long, 1.5T petrol engine plus electric motor, all-wheel drive, 115 km of CLTC electric range on a 5.0 L/100 km combined figure. It is the volume seller in markets that tax by engine displacement, because the combustion side is 1,497 cc.

The Sealion 6 (海狮06 EV) is the mid-size electric one: 4,780 mm, 77.8 kWh LFP Blade pack, 605 km CLTC, front-wheel drive, 2,930 mm wheelbase. This is the unit that competes on paper with a Tesla Model Y, and at US$19,390 FCA it leaves a dealer a margin that a Model Y import does not.

The Sealion 7 (海狮07 DM-i) is the coupe-roof mid-size SUV: 4,830 mm, 1.5T plus electric, 150 km CLTC, 205 kW system output, 6.7 s to 100 km/h. It is the one dealers photograph for showroom windows.

Read this before you quote a Sealion 7. The Sealion 7 sold in Europe, Australia and Thailand is the Sealion 7 EV on the e-Platform 3.0 Evo architecture. The Sealion 7 we hold for export from Nansha in this quarter is the DM-i 150 km plug-in hybrid, chassis code BYD6476SBEV3. Both are real, both carry the same badge, and they are not interchangeable in a homologation file. If your customer has seen a European review and expects a 4.9-second electric SUV, quote the Sealion 6 EV instead or state the powertrain in writing on the proforma.

Sealion 5: the 1,497cc Answer to Displacement-Based Duty

Most of the markets we ship to do not tax a car on its price. They tax it on engine size, and the brackets usually break at 1,500 cc, 1,800 cc and 2,500 cc. A 1.5T unit displaces 1,497 cc, which is 3 cc under the first break and worth several thousand dollars against a 2.0L rival of the same price. That single fact drives more Sealion 5 orders than the specification sheet does.

The car itself is more capable than its price suggests. All-wheel drive comes from a front petrol engine and a rear e-motor rather than a propshaft, so there is no transfer case to service and no rear differential oil to change. System output is 184 kW (250 hp) with 400 Nm, ground clearance is 215 mm, and the combined consumption figure of 5.0 L/100 km assumes a charged battery. In a market where owners never plug in, budget 6.4–7.2 L/100 km, because the car then runs as a series hybrid with a small buffer.

For a fleet buyer, the arithmetic is straightforward. A 115 km electric range covers a full day of municipal or delivery work in most secondary cities, and the petrol engine removes the charging dependency that kills electric fleet pilots in Nigeria and Iraq. We have shipped this configuration to Lagos, Tema, Mombasa, Basra and Tashkent, and the service items that fail are ordinary: brake pads, cabin filters, tyres and the 12 V auxiliary battery. Nothing on the list needs a BYD-specific diagnostic tool.

Sealion 6 EV: 605 km on Paper, 430 km on a Gulf Highway

The Sealion 6 EV carries a 77.8 kWh LFP Blade pack, 160 kW and 310 Nm through the front wheels, and a 2,930 mm wheelbase that puts it in the same interior class as a Model Y. Charging from 30 to 80 per cent takes about 27 minutes on a DC unit, and the boot measures 579 litres with a 60 litre frunk — a packaging advantage that matters to airport-transfer operators.

The range number needs translation. CLTC is a Chinese test cycle run at low average speed, and 605 km CLTC converts to roughly 490–510 km WLTP. On a Gulf motorway at 120 km/h with the air conditioning at 22 degrees, expect 400–430 km of real range. That is still enough for Dubai–Abu Dhabi–Dubai in a day, and it is not enough for Riyadh–Dammam in summer without a charge stop. Say so to the customer before the order, not after.

Sealion 7: What the Extra US$7,590 Buys

Against the Sealion 5, the larger car asks US$7,590 more FOB and returns 35 km of extra electric range, 21 kW more system power, 50 Nm more torque, a 130 mm longer body and a 2,900 mm wheelbase. The 0–100 km/h figure drops from about 7.9 s to 6.7 s. Nothing in that list is a necessity, and all of it is a sales argument in a showroom where the customer is comparing against a used Land Cruiser Prado at twice the price.

Where the larger Sealion earns its premium is long-distance work. The 150 km electric range means a Dubai–Al Ain commuter runs on electricity four days out of five, and the 1.5T engine handles the fifth without a charging stop. For a dealer, the harder calculation is floorplan cost: at US$22,150 FOB, a three-unit container is US$66,450 of capital tied up for 28–34 days of transit to Jebel Ali. The Sealion 5 turns the same capital into four and a half units, which is why most first orders we see are mixed — two Sealion 5 and one Sealion 7.

One option to decline on purpose: the roof-mounted lidar pod visible on some Chinese-market trims. It adds cost, adds a failure point in a hot climate, and DiPilot navigation features that depend on it are geo-locked to Chinese roads. Order the camera-and-radar trim for export.

Read the bottom line with three caveats. The Nigerian figure excludes the SONCAP certificate, which adds US$900–1,400 per consignment rather than per unit, and it assumes a 20 per cent duty line; a pick-up body or a vehicle over 2.5 litres moves to a different line entirely. The Australian duty is zero only with a valid ChAFTA certificate of origin and matching rule-of-origin declaration — without it the general rate applies, and the GST is calculated on the higher figure. And the UAE 5 per cent duty is the GCC common external tariff; a free-zone registration changes where VAT is charged, not whether it is.

Why we do not quote a single “landed price”. Freight moves weekly and duty lines change with a customs circular, not with a season. Treat these as planning figures for November 2026 and ask your clearing agent to confirm the current rate before you print a retail price.

Charging, Service and the PHEV Advantage in Weak-Grid Markets

The Sealion 7 and Sealion 5 both burn petrol, which is the entire point in Lagos, Accra, Mombasa and Baghdad. A PHEV with 115 km of electric range is a fuel-saving device that happens to be a hybrid, not an electric car that needs infrastructure. Owners charge from a wall socket overnight, run the day on electricity where they can, and never depend on a public charger existing.

The Sealion 6 EV is the opposite proposition. It needs a 7 kW AC point at home or office to be tolerable and a DC charger on any inter-city route. In the UAE that is realistic; in Nigeria it is a commitment. Before we accept an order for the Sealion 6 EV into a market we have not shipped to, we ask for the address of the nearest DC charger and the customer's daily distance. Twice this year that question changed the order to a Sealion 7.

On service, the wearable items are generic. Brake pads for the Sealion 7 are the same shape as the ones we wholesale for the Tang family, cabin and air filters cross to standard sizes, and the 1.5T engine uses a conventional oil filter. Consumables come out of our parts finder at Guangzhou pricing, which is a real cost advantage over a dealer paying OEM list in Dubai. What you cannot substitute is the high-voltage side: pack repairs, motor controllers and DC-DC units stay with a BYD-authorised workshop.

Homologation Paperwork by Market

Every destination asks for a conformity document, and no two are the same document. Getting this wrong is more expensive than getting the price wrong, because the car has already sailed.

GCC states: GSO conformity plus SABER

A GSO conformity certificate issued under the Gulf technical regulation, plus the model registration held by the importer. Saudi Arabia adds SASO registration through the SABER platform and an energy-efficiency label; the UAE requires the certificate to be lodged before the bill of lading is issued if you want to avoid a re-inspection fee at Jebel Ali.

Nigeria: SONCAP before the vessel sails

SONCAP is the gate. You need a Product Certificate for the model before shipment and a Certificate of Conformity per consignment, issued after the goods are inspected in China. Form M and a PAAR are handled by the buyer's bank and agent. A vehicle arriving without SONCAP clearance can be stuck at Tin Can for weeks, and storage there is charged daily.

Australia: the market we will not promise

This is the one market where a volume new-car import is effectively closed to an independent exporter. Fremantle requires an entry on the Road Vehicle Australian Register with an approval number covering ADR compliance for the model. Without a full-volume approval held by the manufacturer's Australian distributor, a Sealion 7 can only enter under the specialist enthusiast scheme, and that does not permit resale in volume. If a customer in Perth wants a Sealion 7, the honest answer is that a Chinese independent exporter cannot help — and being honest about that is worth more than the order.

Kenya, Tanzania, Ghana, Algeria and Egypt

Kenya wants a KEBS pre-export verification of conformity; Tanzania and Ghana accept a comparable inspection certificate; Algeria and Egypt route through their own model approvals, and neither is a fast file. We keep a market-by-market checklist and issue it with every proforma.

Container or RoRo: the Freight Decision

RoRo is the default for one or two units. The car is driven on, lashed, and driven off, there is no racking charge and no risk of transit damage from a badly built frame. Nansha to Jebel Ali runs 22–26 days, to Lagos 34–40 days, to Fremantle 18–22 days, and sailing frequency on the Gulf and West Africa routes is roughly weekly.

Container becomes cheaper from three units upward and is the only option when the shipment includes spare parts, because a container can carry brake pads and filters alongside two vehicles and RoRo cannot carry cargo at all. The trade-off is handling: a racked container needs a forklift at destination and a receiver who knows how to de-rack, and damage claims on racked loads are harder to win than on RoRo damage, where the carrier's tally is documented.

Our default for a first order is RoRo for one or two units and a 40HQ with rack from three. For a dealer ordering six or more, we split across two containers rather than load a single unit into a shared container booking, because part-container vehicle shipments attract consolidation fees that erase the saving.

What Actually Fails in the First Twelve Months

Field notes from units we have shipped and followed up, in order of frequency. None of these are design faults; they are the wear items and the software irritations that a dealer should price into the warranty reserve.

  1. 12 V auxiliary battery. The most common first-year claim on both PHEVs. Cars that sit in a yard for eight weeks before delivery arrive with a sulphated battery. We now fit a fresh auxiliary battery on dispatch for Gulf and African orders.
  2. DiLink infotainment region lock. Chinese-market firmware routes some services to domestic servers. Export firmware must be flashed before shipment, and OTA updates afterwards may need to be deferred. Ask for the export build in writing.
  3. Navigation maps. The bundled map data covers China. Either the customer uses a phone, or the dealer loads a local navigation app. Budget for it in the handover, not in the warranty.
  4. Front tyre wear on the Sealion 7. 450 Nm through an all-wheel-drive system with a heavy front axle wears the outside shoulder at around 28,000 km in hot climates. Rotate at 10,000 km and check alignment at delivery.
  5. Charge-port flap and cable seal. Dust ingress in Gulf conditions. A five-dollar seal kit prevents a two-hundred-dollar flap replacement.
  6. Air-conditioning performance after a dusty season. Cabin filter and condenser clean at 15,000 km, not the 30,000 km in the Chinese service schedule.

We publish this list because a dealer who knows the first-year claims can price a service contract, and a dealer who does not will absorb them. It is also the difference between a supplier who ships and a supplier who supports.

Sourcing the Right Sealion for Your Market

Three questions decide the model. What does your customs tariff bracket on — price, displacement or emissions? Does your customer have a charger at home or at the office? And what is your floorplan cost per unit per month? A market that taxes by displacement and has no charging infrastructure wants the Sealion 5 at US$14,560 FOB. A Gulf city with 7 kW points everywhere wants the Sealion 6 EV or the DM-i 150 km car, and the choice between them comes down to whether the buyer wants range or presence.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a BYD Sealion 7 cost FOB Nansha in 2026?
US$22,150 for the DM-i 150 km Leading Edition, against an FCA Guangzhou invoice of US$21,755. The gap covers inland trucking to Nansha, export declaration and terminal handling. At three units in a 40HQ the difference is about US$400 per car; on a single unit it can reach US$520.

Is the Sealion 7 sold for export a PHEV or a BEV?
Both exist. The European and Australian Sealion 7 is a battery-electric car on the e-Platform 3.0 Evo. The Sealion 7 we hold for export this quarter is the DM-i 150 km plug-in hybrid, chassis code BYD6476SBEV3. Confirm the powertrain on the proforma, because the two are not interchangeable in a homologation file.

What is the Sealion 7 landed cost in the UAE?
About US$26,051 at Jebel Ali on a November 2026 cost base: US$22,150 FOB, US$1,120 freight, US$78 insurance, 5 per cent duty of US$1,167, 5 per cent VAT of US$1,226 and roughly US$310 of clearing. That excludes inland delivery and any dealer preparation.

Can I import a BYD Sealion 5 DM-i into Nigeria, and what paperwork does it need?
Yes, at a 20 per cent duty line and 15 per cent VAT, which puts the landed cost near US$34,800 at Lagos before SONCAP. You need a Product Certificate for the model before shipment, a Certificate of Conformity per consignment, Form M and a PAAR. Expect US$900–1,400 for the certificate per consignment, not per vehicle.

Sealion 7 or Sealion 5 — which should a dealer stock first?
Sealion 5 for a first order in almost every case. It costs US$7,590 less FOB, sits in the cheapest displacement band, and turns the same capital into four and a half units instead of three. A mixed order of two Sealion 5 and one Sealion 7 gives the showroom a hero car without tying up the floorplan.

Does the Sealion 6 EV really cover 605 km?
On the CLTC cycle, yes. That converts to roughly 490–510 km WLTP and 400–430 km at a steady 120 km/h with air conditioning. Quote the motorway figure to customers, not the badge figure, or the first long trip becomes a warranty argument.

How many Sealion units fit in a 40HQ container?
Two flat, or three on a two-tier rack at about US$180 extra per car. All three models are 1,905–1,925 mm wide, so nothing loads two abreast inside a 2,352 mm container. Racking requires a forklift at destination.

What is the minimum order and lead time?
One unit, MOQ is one. Lead time is 4–8 weeks ex-Guangzhou depending on colour and trim, plus 10–14 days in peak season if the booking needs a racked container.
