Failure Diagnosis: When to Replace Hub Bearings & ABS Sensors

Replace Hub Bearings & ABS Sensors

Hub bearing failure follows a predictable pattern. The first symptom is usually a humming or droning noise at speeds above 60 km/h that increases with vehicle speed. As the bearing degrades further, the noise becomes a grinding or growling sound, and vibration can be felt through the steering wheel or floor pan. In the final stage, the wheel develops play — detectable by jacking up the vehicle and rocking the wheel at the 12 and 6 o'clock positions.

ABS sensor failure is harder to diagnose because the sensor itself does not make noise. The most common symptom is an illuminated ABS warning light on the dashboard, accompanied by a stored trouble code (C1201-C1299 on Hyundai-Kia vehicles). Other indicators include: traction control activating unexpectedly on dry roads, speedometer fluctuations at low speed, and transmission harshness (some Hyundai-Kia 6-speed automatics use the rear ABS sensor for output shaft speed calculation).

The critical pairing issue: when a Gen 2 or Gen 3 hub bearing with an integrated ABS encoder ring is replaced, the old ABS sensor must be removed from the old bearing and reinstalled in the new one. During this process, the sensor tip can be contaminated with metallic debris from the worn bearing, causing immediate ABS light illumination after the repair. FAQ: Hyundai Kia Hub Bearing & ABS Sensor Wholesale

How long do Hyundai Kia wheel hub bearings last?

OEM-grade hub bearings typically last 100,000-150,000 km under normal driving conditions. In markets with poor road surfaces (unpaved roads, potholes), high ambient temperatures (Middle East, Africa), and heavy vehicle loading (taxis, ride-share), the service life drops to 60,000-90,000 km. Gen 3 bolt-on bearings tend to last longer than Gen 2 press-in bearings because the factory-set preload eliminates installation error. We recommend inspecting hub bearings at every tire rotation (10,000 km) and replacing them at the first sign of noise — continuing to drive on a failed bearing can damage the CV joint, steering knuckle, and ABS sensor tone ring.

What are the symptoms of a failing ABS wheel speed sensor?

The most common symptom is an illuminated ABS warning light with a stored trouble code (C1201-C1299 on Hyundai-Kia). Other signs include: intermittent traction control activation on dry roads, speedometer needle fluctuation at low speeds (below 20 km/h), transmission harshness or delayed shifting (on Hyundai-Kia 6-speed automatics that use the rear wheel speed sensor for output shaft speed), and disabled hill-start assist. To diagnose, use an OBD-II scanner to read the ABS module codes, then check the suspect sensor's resistance (passive: 1,000-2,500 ohms; active: 12V supply with digital output) and inspect the sensor tip for metallic debris contamination from a worn hub bearing.

Should I replace the ABS sensor when replacing the hub bearing?

Yes, for vehicles over 100,000 km. The ABS sensor sits inside the hub bearing's magnetic encoder ring, and when the bearing wears, metallic debris from the worn race and balls contaminates the sensor tip. Removing the sensor from the old bearing and reinstalling it in the new one often results in immediate ABS light illumination because the debris cannot be fully cleaned from the Hall-effect IC. The sensor costs $12-$48 (depending on grade and quantity) versus $15-$85 for the bearing — replacing both at the same time saves the labor cost of a second disassembly and ensures the ABS system functions correctly after the repair.

What is the difference between 51720-1G000 and 51720-2H000?

Both are front wheel hub bearings for the Hyundai Elantra / Kia Cerato platform, but they fit different model years. 51720-1G000 is for the Hyundai Elantra YD/HD (2006-2010) and Kia Cerato TD (2006-2011) with 4-bolt pattern (4×100). 51720-2H000 is for the Hyundai Elantra MD (2011-2015) and Kia Cerato YD (2012-2016) with 5-bolt pattern (5×114.3). 51720-A7000 is the latest version for the Elantra AD (2016-2020) and Cerato BD (2017-2021). All three use a Gen 2 double-row angular contact bearing with integrated ABS encoder ring, but the bolt pattern, hub diameter, and tone ring polarity differ — they are not interchangeable.

Yes. All products carry a 12-month warranty against manufacturing defects (bearing race cracking, ball spalling, seal failure, sensor IC malfunction).
