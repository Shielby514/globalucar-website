It sits inside the fuel tank and delivers gasoline at 350-450 kPa (50-65 psi) to the fuel rail. Typical failure: 80,000-120,000 km in hot-climate markets where fuel quality varies.

Quality verification checklist for fuel pump assemblies:

  • Flow rate test: minimum 80 L/hr at rated pressure (MOBIS spec)
  • Pressure hold test: no drop >5% within 30 minutes at 400 kPa
  • Filter sock integrity: 30-micron pre-filter mesh intact
  • Sender unit resistance range: 10-180 Ω (dry-to-full sweep)
  • Lock ring compatibility: verify fuel tank mounting flange diameter

Hyundai and Kia use Denso and Keihin-sourced port fuel injectors across the Gamma, Nu and Theta engine families. Each injector delivers 70-120 mL/min at rail pressure, controlled by the ECU at 12-16 Ω coil resistance. Injector failure manifests as rough idle, 5-15% fuel economy loss, and cylinder-specific misfire codes (P0301-P0304).

    3. Throttle Body — Air Intake Regulation

    Regulation

    All Hyundai and Kia vehicles from 2012 onward use electronic throttle bodies (ETB) with DC motor actuators and integrated throttle position sensors (TPS). Common bore diameters: 52-68 mm depending on engine displacement. Throttle body contamination causes idle instability (P0507), hesitation on acceleration, and "limp mode" throttle restriction.

    4. Fuel Filter — Contamination Barrier

    ntamination Barrier

    Hyundai and Kia fuel filters come in two types: inline filters (under-vehicle, metal canister) and in-tank filters (integrated with pump module). Inline filters use 5-10 micron paper media rated at 300-400 kPa burst pressure. Replacement interval: 40,000-60,000 km depending on fuel quality (shorter in markets with higher sediment content like Nigeria and Kenya).

    6. Hot-Climate Fuel Parts — What Fails First in Africa & Middle East

    Fails First in Africa & Middle East

    In markets where ambient temperatures exceed 40°C and fuel may sit in tanks for extended periods (low-driving-density cities), specific fuel system components fail faster:

    • Fuel pump assemblies: Ethanol-blended gasoline at high temperature accelerates pump brush wear. Expected life drops from 120,000 km (temperate) to 80,000 km (hot climate). Source: SAE Technical Paper 2024-01-1523, Fuel Pump Durability in Tropical Conditions
    • Fuel injectors: Gum and varnish deposits from stagnant fuel clog injector nozzles. UAE and Saudi market data shows injector cleaning demand peaks at 60,000 km vs 90,000 km in European markets.
    • Fuel filters: Sediment content in Nigerian and Kenyan fuel averages 3-5x higher than European norms. Replace at 30,000-40,000 km instead of 60,000 km.
