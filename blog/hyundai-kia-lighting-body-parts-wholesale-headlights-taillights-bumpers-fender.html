Hyundai Kia Lighting & Body Parts Wholesale: Headlights, Taillights, Bumpers, Fenders, Mirrors & Grilles Sourcing Guide 2026

Published June 30, 2026 by Kingmay Auto Parts — Wholesale auto parts division of Globalucar Group, Guangzhou, China.

Collision repair shops, eBay resellers, and body shops across the Middle East and Africa import more lighting and body parts than any other category for Hyundai and Kia vehicles. A single fender-bender on a Hyundai Elantra or Kia Sportage requires a headlight, bumper cover, fender, grille, and sometimes a side mirror — and buyers need all five parts from one supplier, in the correct paint code, at a price that leaves margin after shipping and import duty.

This guide from Kingmay Auto Parts covers the seven highest-volume lighting and body part categories for Hyundai and Kia vehicles (2008-2025): projector and halogen headlights, LED taillights, fog lights, front bumper covers, fenders, side mirror assemblies, and front grilles. We list 28 OE part numbers with FOB Guangzhou pricing, explain the MOBIS Genuine vs OEM-Grade vs Aftermarket quality framework, and provide a full-container mix recommendation for distributors serving collision repair markets.

1. Why Lighting & Body Parts Are the #1 Collision Repair Category

In markets like Saudi Arabia, UAE, Nigeria, and Kenya, Hyundai Elantra, Tucson, and Kia Sportage are among the top 10 best-selling vehicles. Road conditions, parking habits, and traffic density mean minor front-end and side damage is the most common insurance claim. A typical collision repair order includes:

  • 1 headlight assembly (LH or RH) — most damaged part in frontal impact
  • 1 front bumper cover — cracked or scratched in 80% of front-end hits
  • 1 fender (LH or RH) — dented or folded in side-angle impacts
  • 1 grille — often broken when bumper is pushed in
  • 1 side mirror assembly — clipped in narrow street parking

For eBay resellers, headlights and side mirrors are the highest-margin items because they are small enough for DHL air freight ($6-9/kg) yet command retail prices of $120-$350 per unit. Bumper covers and fenders are better suited for container shipping due to their size.

2. Headlight Assemblies — Projector LED vs Halogen

gen

Hyundai and Kia use two main headlight types depending on trim level and model year:

  • Halogen reflector type (2008-2015 models) — single bulb, chrome reflector housing, cheaper to replace ($45-85 FOB)
  • Projector with LED DRL (2016-2025 models) — projector low beam, LED daytime running light strip, premium appearance ($95-185 FOB)
  • Full LED type (2019+ high trims) — all-LED array, auto-leveling, most expensive ($180-320 FOB)

Assemblies

Taillights are damaged less frequently than headlights but command similar retail prices. Most 2016+ Hyundai and Kia models use LED taillight arrays that cannot be repaired — the entire assembly must be replaced if even one LED segment fails.

Q: What is the difference between MOBIS Genuine and OEM-Grade headlights?

MOBIS Genuine headlights come in Hyundai/Kia branded packaging from the MOBIS factory with full warranty backing (typically 12 months). OEM-Grade headlights are produced in the same IATF 16949-certified factory using the same mold and materials, but are sold unbranded (no MOBIS logo on the lens or housing) at 40-50% lower cost. For independent body shops and eBay resellers, OEM-Grade is the recommended choice because it offers identical fitment and light output at a price that preserves resale margin.
