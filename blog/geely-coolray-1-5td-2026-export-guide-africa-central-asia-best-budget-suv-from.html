3. BMA Platform Engineering: Geely-Volvo Shared ArchitectureShared Architecture

The Coolray is built on Geely's BMA (B-segment Modular Architecture) platform — the same engineering framework used for the Geely Icon, Geely Azkarra and Proton X50 (Malaysia). BMA was developed by Geely's CEVT (China Euro Vehicle Technology) engineering centre in Gothenburg, Sweden, leveraging Volvo-parented safety architecture principles:

  • 66% high-strength steel body structure: A-pillars, B-pillars, roof cross-members and door reinforcement beams use 1,500 MPa ultra-high-strength steel — the same grade specified in Volvo XC40 side-impact protection zones. This material rating exceeds Japanese and Korean competitors that typically specify 980–1,180 MPa in equivalent zones.
  • Integrated body cage: The BMA platform uses a continuous ring structure from A-pillar through C-pillar, distributing impact energy across 8 load paths rather than 4–5 typical in subcompact SUVs.
  • Front crumple zone: Progressive deformation zone with three-stage energy absorption — thin-wall aluminium crush initiator, medium-strength steel transition zone and high-strength steel cabin barrier. Engine displacement zone provides 120 mm additional crush distance beyond the 1.5T engine block.

For fleet buyers and government procurement agencies evaluating crash safety, the BMA platform's high-strength steel percentage and multi-load-path architecture meet UN ECE R94/R95 front and side impact requirements, which are the regulatory standard in Nigeria, Kenya
