Why TPMS Is the Fastest-Growing Replacement Part Category

Tire pressure monitoring sensors stopped being optional hardware a decade ago. The United States mandated TPMS on all light vehicles from 2007, the European Union followed in 2014, and China's GB 26149 standard made the system compulsory on every new passenger car from January 2020. That single regulatory wave created a replacement fleet measured in hundreds of millions of vehicles — and every one of those sensors carries an internal battery with a service life of 5 to 10 years.

The arithmetic is what makes TPMS attractive for wholesale distributors. A sedan sold in 2018 is now entering its second or third sensor replacement cycle. A four-sensor set at $6.00 FOB represents $24 of goods that retails for $120-200 fitted in most markets, and the part is small enough that a single carton can hold several hundred units. For repair shops, TPMS service has become a standard upsell attached to every tire replacement and seasonal wheel change.

Kingmay's Guangzhou inventory covers the three platforms that dominate export-destination fleets: Toyota Corolla / Camry / RAV4, Nissan Sentra / Sylphy / X-Trail / Qashqai, and Hyundai Elantra / Tucson / Sonata / Kia Sportage / K5. All sensors are packaged with valve stems, seals and nuts where applicable, labeled with OE-grade references, and available from 50-piece trial orders up to full mixed-container loads.

Direct vs Indirect TPMS: What Distributors Should Stock

Not every tire pressure system uses a physical sensor in the wheel. Understanding the split matters because it determines which part numbers actually move in your market.

System type How it works Typical vehicles Parts sold
Direct TPMSPressure sensor inside each wheel transmits by RF to the body control moduleMost US-spec vehicles, all post-2020 Chinese-market cars, most Korean and Japanese models for GCC and Latin AmericaSensor units, valve stems, service kits
Indirect TPMS (iTPMS)Uses ABS wheel-speed data to infer pressure loss; no sensor in the wheelMany European-market Hyundai and Kia models, some budget trimsNo dedicated sensor — only ABS sensors and module software

For wholesale buyers the practical takeaway is simple: stock direct-TPMS sensors for the models your customers actually service. Toyota, Nissan, Hyundai and Kia passenger cars sold across the Middle East, Africa, Latin America and Southeast Asia overwhelmingly use direct systems with replaceable sensor units, which is why those four brands form the core of the Kingmay TPMS catalog.

315 MHz vs 433 MHz: Matching Frequency to Destination Market

TPMS sensors transmit on one of two radio bands, and the frequency is fixed by the vehicle's body control module — a 433 MHz sensor will never pair with a 315 MHz car. Buying the wrong band is the most expensive mistake a first-time TPMS importer makes, because it only shows up after the customer has mounted the tires.

Frequency Primary markets Typical vehicle sourcing
315 MHzUnited States, Canada, Middle East (GCC-spec), Latin America, China domesticToyota, Nissan, Hyundai and Kia models built for or exported to those regions
433 MHzEurope, Russia, Central Asia, most of Africa, Southeast Asia, AustraliaEuropean and Asian-market variants of the same nameplates

Because the same Corolla or Tucson can carry either band depending on where it was originally sold, the practical rule for distributors in mixed markets is to stock both frequencies in the top ten references and verify the band against the vehicle VIN or the existing sensor part number before shipping. Kingmay marks the frequency on every carton and on each sensor label to prevent warehouse mix-ups.

Pre-Programmed vs Programmable Universal Sensors

The catalog splits into two product families, and the right choice depends on how your customers work.

Type How it works Best for Watch-outs
Pre-programmed (OE-fitment)Sensor is written with the exact vehicle protocol at the factory; bolt on and it pairs automatically or after a relearnDealers, fleet workshops, distributors serving one brand at volumeEach part number is vehicle-specific — stock the references your market actually runs
Programmable / universalBlank sensor loaded with the target vehicle protocol using a TPMS programming tool before fittingMulti-brand repair shops, mobile tire services, distributors with diverse VIORequires the programming tool and protocol database to be kept current

Pre-programmed sensors win on installation speed and zero-tool cost, which is why they dominate dealer and fleet business. Programmable universal sensors win on inventory efficiency: a workshop that services Toyota, Nissan, Hyundai and Kia from one shelf can hold a single universal SKU instead of four brand-specific ones, then write the correct protocol in about 30 seconds. Most Kingmay wholesale customers stock a base of top-selling pre-programmed references and add programmable units as flexible coverage for the long tail.

Failure Diagnosis: When to Replace TPMS Sensors

Most TPMS complaints trace back to four root causes. Training workshop customers to diagnose before replacing protects their margins and prevents warranty claims on healthy units.

Symptom Most likely cause Action
Warning light on one corner, no pressure lossSensor battery dead (typical after 5-10 years)Replace the sensor unit; battery is sealed and not serviceable
Slow leak around the valveGrommet or seal aged, valve core corrodedRenew service kit and valve core; sensor body reusable if battery is healthy
Light on after tire rotation or replacementSensor positions not relearned to the moduleRun the vehicle relearn procedure or reprogram with a TPMS tool
Light after wheel impact (pothole, curb strike)Sensor body cracked or broken off the stemReplace the sensor; inspect rim for damage
Intermittent signal, cold-weather onlyMarginal battery voltage drops in coldReplace proactively — failure will become permanent within months

Because the battery is sealed inside the sensor body, "replace the whole unit" is the standard service model, and that is exactly what makes the category recurring. A workshop that fits forty sets of tires a month at three years of average vehicle age is a guaranteed repeat buyer of sensors, and the service-kit line turns every seasonal tire change into another order.

FAQ: TPMS Sensor Sourcing from China

Can I mix 315 MHz and 433 MHz sensors in one order?

Yes. Mixed-frequency containers are standard practice for distributors serving regions where both bands circulate — the Gulf, East Africa and Latin America all run 315 MHz on newer imports and 433 MHz on European-market vehicles. Kingmay packs each frequency in clearly marked cartons and lists the split on the packing list so destination customs clearance stays clean.

How do I verify which frequency a customer's vehicle uses?

Two reliable methods. First, check the existing sensor part number through the valve stem — the number is printed on the sensor body and maps directly to a frequency. Second, match the vehicle VIN or model-year market: North America, GCC and China-market vehicles are almost always 315 MHz, while European, Russian, most African and Southeast Asian market vehicles are 433 MHz. When in doubt, ask the customer to read the number off an existing sensor before ordering.

Do programmable TPMS sensors need a special tool to install?

The tool is needed to write the vehicle protocol into the blank sensor before fitting, not to mount the sensor itself. The programming step takes about 30 seconds per sensor with a handheld tool. Workshops that already own a TPMS diagnostic tool usually have programming built in; those that do not can add the Kingmay handheld tool at container-tier pricing, which is why most wholesale orders include one or two tools per pallet of sensors.

What is the typical battery life of a TPMS sensor?

Five to ten years of real-world service, depending on how often the vehicle is driven and the climate. High ambient heat shortens lithium battery life, which is why sensors in GCC vehicles often fail at the five-year mark while the same reference in a temperate climate runs past eight years. The battery is sealed inside the sensor body and is not field-serviceable, so the replacement unit is the service part.

Are aftermarket TPMS sensors as reliable as OE units?

OE-grade aftermarket units from a qualified factory match the OE pressure range, transmission protocol and battery specification, and are validated against the same relearn procedures. The differences from genuine-packaged parts are packaging, branding and price — typically 40-60% lower at wholesale. For independent repair shops and distributors, OE-grade aftermarket is the standard choice; genuine-packaged units are sourced for dealership customers who require original boxes.

What documents come with a wholesale TPMS order?

Each shipment includes a commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin and a pre-shipment inspection report with photos. Sensors ship as HS 8531.90 or 9031.80 depending on destination, and Kingmay labels cartons with frequency, part number and quantity for straightforward customs clearance. Radio-frequency certification documents (CE for 433 MHz, FCC for 315 MHz) are available on request for regulated markets.
