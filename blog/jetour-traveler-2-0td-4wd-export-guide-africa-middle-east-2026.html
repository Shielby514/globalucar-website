Jetour Traveler 2.0TD 4WD 2026 Export Guide: China's Body-on-Frame Adventure SUV at $22,208 FOB Nansha

📅 2026-06-26 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Globalucar Editorial
Quick Answer: Complete export guide for the 2026 Jetour Traveler 2.0TD 4WD Explorer Edition — 254 hp body-on-frame adventure SUV, 8-speed AT, XWD intelligent 4WD with 8 drive modes, 220 mm ground clearance, 700 mm wading depth, FOB Nansha $22,208. The Jetour Traveler 2.0TD 4WD Explorer Edition is Chery Group's body-on-frame adventure SUV — a 254 hp, eight-speed automatic, ladder-chassis 4x4 with 220 mm ground clearance and 700 mm wading depth. At FOB Nansha $22,208, it undercuts the Toyota Fortuner 2.8 GD-6 4WD by approximately 40% while matching or exceeding its off-road specifications. For importers in Africa and the Middle East looking for a capable, well-equipped 4WD at half the dealer price of Japanese equivalents, the Traveler is the most competitive body-on-frame SUV currently shipping from China.

1. Why the Jetour Traveler Matters — Body-on-Frame at a Crossover Price

The global 4x4 market is dominated by three vehicles: the Toyota Fortuner, Mitsubishi Pajero Sport, and Ford Everest. Each rides on a body-on-frame (BOF) ladder chassis — the same architecture that underpins pickup trucks — and each retails for $35,000-$45,000 in GCC and African markets. The Jetour Traveler delivers the same BOF architecture with comparable or better off-road hardware at FOB $22,208.

What makes this possible is Chery Group's manufacturing scale. Jetour is Chery's dedicated youth/adventure brand, and the Traveler shares its platform with the Chery Tiggo 8 PRO and Jetour X70 series, all produced at Chery's Wuhu mega-factory with 1.2 million units annual capacity. Platform sharing + high-volume production = body-on-frame capability at crossover pricing.

Key differentiator: Unlike the Haval Dargo and Tank 300 — which are also Chinese 4WD SUVs but use monocoque construction with bolt-on off-road hardware — the Traveler rides on a genuine ladder chassis with a solid rear axle. This matters for buyers who need to carry heavy loads on unpaved roads, tow trailers, and operate in regions where independent rear suspension components fail under constant abuse.

The Traveler's XWD (X-Mode All-Wheel Drive) system is not a basic on-demand AWD that sends 10% torque to the rear when the front wheels slip. It is a full-time intelligent system with eight selectable drive modes and a mechanical locking function that distributes torque across all four wheels. The eight drive modes are: Sport, Normal, Eco, Snow, Mud, Sand, Rock, and X-MODE — the last being a fully automatic terrain-response mode that reads steering angle, wheel slip, throttle position and incline to determine optimal torque split in real time.

This feature is standard on Land Cruiser 300 and Land Rover Defender at $60,000+ price points. On the Traveler, it comes factory-installed at FOB $22,208.

Trail-capability assessment: The 255/55 R20 tire size means the Traveler ships on 20-inch alloy wheels with highway-terrain rubber. For buyers operating on heavily rutted tracks or rocky terrain, we recommend replacing with 265/65 R17 all-terrain tires (aftermarket, available through Globalucar parts channel). The 20-inch setup is ideal for markets where the vehicle splits duty between paved roads and occasional dirt — which describes most African and Middle Eastern use cases outside dedicated safari/tour ope

The diesel question: The Fortuner and Pajero Sport have a fuel-economy edge (7.8-8.0 L/100 km vs 9.2 L/100 km) and higher torque figures thanks to turbo-diesel engines. However, diesel fuel quality in many African markets is inconsistent — high-sulfur diesel clogs DPFs and damages high-pressure common-rail injection systems. In Nigeria and Kenya, where diesel sulfur content can exceed 500 ppm (vs 10-15 ppm in Europe), petrol engines like the Traveler's 2.0T avoid the DPF-replacement headache that costs $1,200-2,500 on modern diesel SUVs.       Q: Is the Jetour Traveler a genuine body-on-frame SUV?

      Yes. The Jetour Traveler rides on a ladder-frame chassis with a solid rear axle — the same architecture used by the Toyota Fortuner, Mitsubishi Pajero Sport, and Ford Everest. It is not a monocoque crossover with bolt-on off-road styling. The chassis code SQR6481T1JCBG confirms body-on-frame construction in Chery's MIIT filings.

      Q: How does the Traveler compare to the Toyota Fortuner for African buyers?

      The Traveler offers 254 hp vs 204 hp (Fortuner diesel), an 8-speed AT vs 6-speed AT, a 15.6-inch Snapdragon 8155 infotainment system vs 9-inch display, Sony 12-speaker audio vs 6-speaker, and full ADAS suite (ACC, LKA, AEB, 360 camera) vs basic pre-collision warning — all at $22,208 FOB vs approximately $36,800 GCC retail for a comparable Fortuner 4WD. The Fortuner's advantages: diesel fuel economy (7.8 vs 9.2 L/100 km), proven resale value, and Toyota's service network in Africa.

      Q: What is the warranty on the Jetour Traveler for export markets?

      Chery provides a 5-year / 100,000 km manufacturer warranty on the Jetour Traveler through authorized export channels. This covers powertrain (engine, transmission, drivetrain), electrical systems, and body corrosion. The warranty is honored through Chery's export service network, which includes authorized distributors in 30+ African and Middle Eastern countries.

      Q: Can I import a single unit or do I need a minimum order?

      Globalucar accepts MOQ 1 unit. The vehicle ships in a shared 40ft HQ container (2 units per container). For single-unit orders, Globalucar pairs your unit with another buyer's vehicle in the same container. RORO shipping is available for 6+ unit orders at a lower per-unit cost.
