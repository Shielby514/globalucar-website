Chery Tiggo 8 Pro / Tiggo 9 2026 Export Guide: FOB Nansha Prices for Russia & Central Asia

Why Russian and Central Asian Importers Buy the Tiggo 8 Series

The Chery Tiggo 8 has become the most requested Chinese SUV in the Russia-bound export trade, and the numbers behind that position are easy to check. Chery has held the top spot among Chinese brands in the Russian market since 2023, with the Tiggo 7 Pro and Tiggo 8 Pro Max regulars in the country's ten best-selling SUV charts (AEB registration data, 2026). For a wholesaler in Moscow, Yekaterinburg or Almaty, the Tiggo 8 Pro packages a 7-seat family SUV with a 1.6T turbo engine, a 2,710 mm wheelbase and a parts network Chery has spent five years building across the Eurasian Economic Union.

The arbitrage that drives the Chery Tiggo 8 export business is the retail-to-cost spread. Russian dealers list the Tiggo 8 Pro and Pro Max at roughly 2.3 to 2.8 million rubles — about $31,500 to $38,400 at the 2026 exchange rate near 73 RUB/USD. The same vehicle leaves Nansha Port at less than half of that figure, which is why licensed exporters, parallel-import dealers and fleet buyers all compete for allocation of this one model line.

In Central Asia the story is the same with a different calendar. Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan share Russia's EAC certification framework as EAEU members, Uzbekistan has been cutting import duties on passenger vehicles since 2023, and Chery's contract assembly in Karaganda plus dealer networks in Almaty, Astana and Tashkent have normalized parts supply. A Tiggo 9 bought at FOB Nansha and cleared in Almaty or Tashkent competes directly with Russian-market retail — at a shorter shipping leg.

Tiggo 8 Pro vs Tiggo 9: Full Specification Comparison

The two vehicles serve two different missions. The Tiggo 8 Pro is the volume product: a mid-size 7-seat SUV with the 1.6T SQRF4J16 engine, built for families and ride-hailing fleets, tuned to run on 92 RON fuel and priced for markets where $15,000-class FOB still buys real equipment. The Tiggo 9 is the flagship: Chery's 2.0T Kunpeng engine with 254 hp, an 8-speed automatic on export trims, and the chassis dimensions that let it sell against the Haval H9 and Hyundai Santa Fe at retail. Russia Import Cost: Duty, VAT and the Utilization Fee

This is the section where budgets slip, because the tax stack on a vehicle entering Russia in 2026 involves four separate charges plus service fees. The worked example below follows a Tiggo 8 Pro 1.6T Luxury arriving at Vladivostok with a CIF value of $15,600 (FOB $13,700 plus roughly $1,900 RoRo freight). The figures come from a broker-cleared 2026 Vladivostok clearing schedule, and they match what independent importing guides are publishing for this engine class this year.

Read against the FOB table above, that stack puts the cleared cost of this Tiggo 8 Pro at roughly $31,800 to $35,200 on the ground in Vladivostok — squarely inside the Russian retail band of $31,500 to $38,400. The margin on the 1.6T FWD car is therefore thin and lives or dies on the utilization-fee coefficient, which changes by federal decree. Professional importers concentrate their volume on the Tiggo 8 Pro Max 2.0T and Tiggo 9 trims instead, where the retail-to-cost gap widens enough to pay for freight, clearance and a dealer margin. Confirm the current coefficients with your Moscow or Vladivostok broker before contracting — we provide the invoice-side numbers, your broker provides the local-side numbers, and the two must be checked together.

On the compliance side, a type-approved model like the Tiggo 8 Pro clears through a documentary EAC declaration rather than full physical testing. Your clearing broker prepares the SBKTS (certificate of vehicle safety construction) and the EPTS (electronic passport) from the export documents we issue. Two localization items surface after clearance: the Chinese-spec infotainment unit needs a region flash for full Russian language at $280–420 in Moscow, and a unit landing on worn 235/55R19 tires adds $95–140 per tire to make it showroom-ready. Both are line items worth booking before the deposit, not after.

Port Choice: Vladivostok, Novorossiysk and the Rail Alternative

Vladivostok handles about three quarters of all China-built SUV imports, and for good reason: RoRo freight from Shanghai or Ningbo runs $1,800–2,400 per vehicle with a 5–8 day transit and three to four sailings per week through 2026. Customs release for a documented Tiggo 8 Pro takes four to eight working days. From there, TransSib rail to Moscow covers the remaining 9,000-plus kilometers in 8–14 days, or trucking does it in 14–21. For Far East, Siberian and Ural buyers, the port is effectively local.

Novorossiysk serves European Russia, Krasnodar, Rostov and the Caucasus with a shorter final leg. The ocean route via the Suez Canal costs $3,800–5,200 per vehicle and takes 28–35 days at one to two sailings per week, with customs release in five to ten days. Choose it when your buyer sits west of the Volga and can wait a month; otherwise the Vladivostok-plus-rail combination usually beats it on total landed time.

Quick answer on routing: Far East, Siberia, Urals → Vladivostok RoRo (5–8 days at sea + rail/truck onward). European Russia → Novorossiysk via Suez (28–35 days) only if your buyer can wait; otherwise Vladivostok + TransSib rail lands a Tiggo 8 in Moscow in under three weeks door to door.

Central Asia: Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan

Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, as EAEU members, apply the same EAC framework and the union's common customs tariff — the duty and utilization-fee logic from the Russia section carries over, administered by local brokers in Almaty, Astana and Bishkek. What changes is the routing: instead of an ocean leg, units move by rail from Guangzhou through the Khorgos/Alashankou land border, roughly 6,000 kilometers in 18–25 days, then clear for the Kazakh market. We covered this corridor in detail in our Toyota Corolla Cross FOB Khorgos guide, and the same math applies to a Tiggo 8 Pro at its lower FOB price.

Uzbekistan sits outside the EAEU but has cut passenger-vehicle import duties in stages since 2023, and Tashkent dealers actively source Chinese SUVs through both direct clearance and Kazakh transit. Kyrgyzstan's historical role as a low-duty re-export hub has narrowed as EAEU enforcement tightened after 2023 — plan to clear in the country of final sale with a local broker rather than routing through Bishkek for paperwork reasons. On warranty: Chery's official Russian network markets a 7-year/200,000 km new-car warranty, but that coverage attaches to officially imported units; export-channel vehicles are supported by Chery's growing parts distribution instead, which is why we include the parts-supplier introduction with every fleet order.

Pre-Shipment Checks That Protect Your Margin

Every one of the post-clearance costs listed earlier can be predicted before the vessel sails. These are the six checks we run on every Tiggo 8 order:

  1. VIN and export chassis verification — the plate, MIIT type approval and invoice must match the licensed export channel exactly.
  2. Service book and stamps — a complete Chery service book protects $1,200–1,800 of resale value in Russia; gaps get documented before shipping.
  3. Tire condition — tread photos on all four 235/55R19 (or 235/45R20) positions plus the spare, so the buyer budgets $0 or $600, never a surprise.
  4. A/C full-load test — 15 minutes at maximum, verifying 5–8°C outlet temperature and no compressor cycling shudder, since an overhaul runs $380–620 in Moscow.
  5. Multimedia region-flash decision — confirmed with the buyer pre-deposit so the $280–420 Moscow flash is either booked or skipped knowingly.
  6. Inspection report with photos — 40+ images covering interior, chassis numbers and any transport marks, issued before the balance payment.

FAQ: Chery Tiggo 8 Export from China

How much does it cost to import a Chery Tiggo 8 Pro to Russia in 2026?

On a broker-cleared example with a $15,600 CIF value at Vladivostok, the total import burden runs $16,200–19,600: customs duty of $5,900–6,300, 20% VAT of $4,300–4,500, a utilization fee of $5,200–7,800 depending on the current coefficient, excise of about $110–125, and $700–900 in broker and documentation fees. That puts the cleared vehicle at roughly $31,800–35,200 on the ground. Coefficients change by federal decree, so confirm with your clearing broker at order time.

What is the Tiggo 8 Pro FOB price at Nansha Port?

Indicative September 2026 FOB Nansha pricing runs from $14,200 for the 1.6T Comfort 5-seat to $15,600 for the 1.6T Luxury 7-seat, with the Tiggo 8 Pro Max 2.0T at about $17,800. The Tiggo 9 starts near $22,500 in 2WD 8AT form and $24,800 for the AWD Flagship. Final quotes depend on trim, color and dealer-stock allocation at the time of deposit.

Vladivostok or Novorossiysk — which Russian port should I choose?

For Far East, Siberian and Ural buyers, Vladivostok: $1,800–2,400 per vehicle by RoRo, 5–8 days at sea, customs release in 4–8 working days, then TransSib rail to Moscow in 8–14 days. For European Russia, Novorossiysk via Suez costs $3,800–5,200 and takes 28–35 days — choose it only when the final destination sits west of the Volga and the buyer can absorb the longer transit.

What documents does a Chinese Chery SUV need at Russian customs?

Four items drive the file: the commercial invoice and bill of lading from the exporter, a documentary EAC declaration (the Tiggo 8 Pro is type-approved, so no physical testing), the SBKTS certificate of vehicle safety construction, and the EPTS electronic passport. Your clearing broker prepares the SBKTS and EPTS from our export documents; budget $700–900 for the service bundle.

Can the Tiggo 9 be exported to Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan?

Yes. Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan apply EAEU rules — the same EAC certification and common customs tariff as Russia — with clearance in Almaty, Astana or Bishkek after an 18–25 day rail leg from Guangzhou through the Khorgos/Alashankou border. Uzbekistan, outside the EAEU, has been cutting passenger-vehicle duties since 2023 and clears directly through Tashkent brokers. Each route needs its own broker-side cost sheet before contracting.

Does the Tiggo 8 Pro come with 7 seats and Russian-language multimedia?

Both seat layouts exist: the Comfort trim ships as a 5-seater, while Luxury and Pro Max trims offer the 5- or 7-seat configuration. The Chinese-spec head unit is Mandarin-first and needs a region flash for full Russian language, costing $280–420 in Moscow — we confirm the flash decision with you before the deposit so it is a planned line item, not a post-sale dispute.
