SEPA 2.0 Architecture & XNGP Autonomous Driving: The Tech Behind the G6

The XPENG G6 is built on the SEPA 2.0 (Smart Electric Platform Architecture), XPENG's second-generation integrated EV platform. Key architectural advantages over first-generation Chinese EV platforms include:

  • CIB (Cell-to-Body) battery integration: The 87.5 kWh NCM battery pack is structurally integrated into the floor pan, eliminating the traditional battery pack enclosure and contributing to a 10% weight saving versus a separate-pack architecture. This also increases torsional rigidity to 41,600 Nm/degree — 25% higher than the Tesla Model Y's 32,000 Nm/degree figure.
  • Front-rear integrated die-casting: The front cabin structure and rear underbody are each produced as single aluminium die-castings (using LK Group 12,000-tonne die-casting machines), eliminating approximately 160 individual stampings and welds per vehicle. This reduces production complexity and improves structural consistency versus multi-piece welded assemblies.
  • 800V SiC power module: The silicon carbide (SiC) inverter operates at up to 99.5% peak efficiency, compared to 96-98% for IGBT-based 400V inverters. The resulting reduction in switching losses directly translates to 3-5% range improvement and reduced thermal load on the cooling system.

The XNGP (XPENG Navigation Guided Pilot) system on the G6 is XPENG's most advanced ADAS suite. The sensor array includes 2 forward-facing LiDAR units (roof-mounted), 12 high-definition cameras (7 x 8MP + 4 x surround + 1 DMS), 5 millimetre-wave radars and 12 ultrasonic sensors. XNGP enables city-level autonomous driving on mapped roads in China and is being expanded to international markets through XPENG's partnership with local HD map providers. The system is a key differentiator for tech-forward Middle East buyers who value autonomous driving capability — the dual LiDAR configuration provides reliable object detection in low-light and dust/sand conditions common in GCC environments, where camera-only systems (Tesla Vision) can struggle.

Q: Can the XPENG G6 use Tesla Superchargers in the Middle East?

The XPENG G6 uses the CCS2 (Combined Charging System Type 2) connector standard, which is the dominant DC fast-charging standard across the Middle East. Tesla Superchargers in the UAE (DEWA Green Charger network) and Saudi Arabia use CCS2 connectors and are physically compatible with the XPENG G6. However, Tesla's Supercharger network currently restricts non-Tesla vehicle access on a station-by-station basis in the Middle East — not all stations are open to third-party vehicles. The G6 can charge at any public CCS2 DC fast charger (DEWA, EVIQ, IONITY, Shell Recharge, etc.) with full 280 kW charging speed at 800V-compatible stations. At standard 400V CCS2 chargers (150 kW), the G6's onboard voltage booster enables charging at the charger's maximum output. For home charging, the G6 supports 11 kW AC (Type 2) with a full charge in approximately 8.5 hours.

Q: What is the XPENG G6 warranty for export vehicles?

XPENG G6 export vehicles carry a 5-year/120,000 km vehicle warranty and an 8-year/160,000 km high-voltage battery warranty (whichever comes first). The battery warranty guarantees at least 70% of original capacity retention at 8 years or 160,000 km. The warranty covers manufacturing defects in materials and workmanship but excludes normal wear items (tyres, brake pads, wiper blades, 12V auxiliary battery) and damage from unauthorised modifications. Warranty service is provided through XPENG's authorised international dealer network where available; in markets without an official dealer, Globalucar facilitates warranty claim processing and parts supply. Contact ucars@global-ucars.com for the complete warranty terms document.

Q: How does shipping an EV like the XPENG G6 differ from shipping a petrol car?

Shipping an EV internationally requires compliance with IMDG (International Maritime Dangerous Goods) Code Class 9 regulations for lithium-ion battery transport. The G6's 87.5 kWh battery pack is classified as UN 3171 (battery-powered vehicle). Key requirements: 1) battery state of charge (SOC) must be between 30-50% for ocean transport (per IMO guidelines to reduce thermal runaway risk), 2) the vehicle must be declared as Dangerous Goods Class 9 on the bill of lading, 3) RORO (roll-on/roll-off) vessels are preferred over container shipping because RORO allows continuous ventilation and fire monitoring, 4) shipping lines may apply a DG surcharge of $150-300 per EV unit. Globalucar handles all IMDG documentation and DG declaration as part of the standard export service — no additional paperwork is required from the importer. The 30-50% SOC requirement means the vehicle will arrive at the destination port with sufficient charge to drive off the vessel but will need charging before delivery to the end customer.
